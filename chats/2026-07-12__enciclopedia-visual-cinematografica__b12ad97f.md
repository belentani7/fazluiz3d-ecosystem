## 🇵🇹 Explicação do projeto (em português)

O prompt abaixo foi concebido como o **mais extenso e completo do mundo** para instruir uma IA a construir uma verdadeira *enciclopédia técnica de referência visual*. Ele não é um prompt de geração de imagens — é um **meta-prompt** que transforma a IA num redator técnico especializado, capaz de produzir material de nível universitário sobre cinematografia, fotografia, VFX e direção de arte.

### Por que este enfoque é superior?
Pedir a uma IA "escreve-me um prompt para IA" gera respostas superficiais. Pedir-lhe **"escreve uma enciclopédia técnica profissional, exaustiva, sem comprimir informação"** obriga-a a aceder ao conhecimento profundo dos seus dados de treino sobre manuais de cinematografia, livros técnicos, white papers de fabricantes e literatura especializada.

### Estrutura recomendada de uso
O projeto foi desenhado para ser gerado em **8 volumes independentes** (conforme sugerido), pois:
1. Um único prompt dessa dimensão excede o limite de contexto de qualquer modelo numa única geração.
2. Cada volume pode ser refinado e expandido individualmente.
3. Permite manter a coerência terminológica entre capítulos.
4. Facilita a revisão humana por especialistas em cada domínio.

### Como executar o master prompt
1. **Copie o bloco** abaixo (em inglês — a maioria dos modelos tem melhor performance técnica nesta língua).
2. **Acrescente ao final uma instrução** do tipo: *"Start with Volume I: Cameras, Sensors, Lenses and Optics. Generate Chapter 1: Professional Cameras."*
3. **Gere capítulo a capítulo**, não volumes inteiros de uma vez.
4. **Consolide** cada capítulo num documento mestre antes de avançar, para manter referências cruzadas.
5. **Itere**: cada capítulo pode ser expandido posteriormente com prompts como *"Expand Chapter X with 50 additional sub-concepts and 30 comparison tables."*

### Recomendações críticas
- **Use modelos com janelas de contexto extensas** (GPT-4o, Claude Opus, Gemini 1.5 Pro) para gerar volumes inteiros com coerência.
- **Não comprima informação**: a instrução "Do not compress" é a parte mais importante do prompt.
- **Mantenha um glossário unificado** à medida que os volumes forem gerados.
- **Valide tecnicamente** cada capítulo com profissionais da área (DOPs, coloristas, artistas de VFX).

### 1.4 Detailed Entries

#### ENTRY 01: ARRI ALEXA 35

**1. Name**
ARRI Alexa 35

**2. Alternative Names**
Alexa 35, A35, The New Flagship, ALEV 4 Camera.

**3. Category / Subcategory**
Digital Cinema Camera / Super 35 Format / High-End Production.

**4. Definition**
The current flagship digital cinema camera from ARRI, featuring the newly developed ALEV 4 sensor and the REVEAL Color Science image processing pipeline. It is the first new sensor architecture ARRI has released in over a decade.

**5. Etymology**
*Alexa*: A proprietary name chosen by ARRI (rumored to reference the Library of Alexandria or the Amazon river, though officially unspecified).
*35*: Refers to the **Super 35** sensor format size, the historical standard for 35mm motion picture film.

**6. Visual Effect**
Produces images with an "organic" and "film-like" quality. Highlights roll off smoothly into white without clipping harshly (a common digital artifact). Skin tones are rendered with exceptional accuracy and subtlety. The image has a "texture" rather than "digital sharpness," often described as having "weight" and "depth."

**7. Physical/Technical Mechanism**
Uses the **ALEV 4** sensor, a 4.6K Super 35 (29.14 x 19.54 mm) CMOS sensor. It employs a new **Dual Conversion Gain** architecture at the pixel level (hardware-based, not software) to simultaneously capture high and low sensitivity signals, combining them to achieve **17 stops of dynamic range**. It utilizes the **REVEAL Color Science** pipeline, which debayers the RAW data with a new algorithm that preserves more resolution and color accuracy than previous generations.

**8. How It Changes the Image**
*   **Highlight Handling:** Allows shooting directly into the sun or bright practical lights without losing detail in the clouds or bulb filaments.
*   **Low Light:** The 17 stops allow for significant underexposure in shadows while retaining clean, noise-free information.
*   **Texture:** The image avoids the "plastic" or "video" look of sharper, smaller sensors. It mimics the grain structure and resolution limit of film stock.

**9. Real-World Origin**
Manufactured by ARRI (Arnold & Richter) in Munich, Germany. Released in 2022.

**10. Historical Context**
Released to replace the legendary Alexa Mini and Alexa XT lineage. It marks the first major sensor update since the original Alexa in 2010, addressing the industry's demand for higher dynamic range and better low-light performance without moving to Large Format.

**11. Level of Realism**
**Cinematic Photorealism.** This is the benchmark against which all other digital cinema cameras are measured.

**12. Visual Characteristics**
*   **Aspect Ratio:** Native 4:3 sensor allows for anamorphic shooting (2.39:1 desqueezed) or spherical 1.85:1/1.66:1.
*   **Color:** Naturalistic, slightly warm/neutral bias, excellent red/green separation.
*   **Noise:** Extremely fine, film-like grain pattern in the shadows (unlike the blocky chroma noise of consumer cameras).

**13. Advantages**
*   Unrivaled dynamic range (17 stops).
*   REVEAL Color Science offers better color accuracy and resolution.
*   Compact form factor (similar to Mini) but with full accessory ecosystem.
*   Industry-standard reliability and rental support.

**14. Disadvantages / Limitations**
*   **Resolution:** 4.6K is lower than competitors (RED 8K, Sony 8K). Not suitable for VFX-heavy work requiring massive cropping or extreme reframing.
*   **Cost:** Extremely high rental/purchase price.
*   **Data Rate:** ARRIRAW files are massive (terabytes per hour), requiring robust DIT (Digital Imaging Technician) workflows.

**15. Typical Use Cases**
High-end feature films, premium TV commercials, music videos, high-budget streaming series (e.g., *Dune: Part Two*, *The Creator*).

**16. Common Mistakes**
*   **Over-lighting:** Beginners often light Alexa 35 scenes too brightly, negating its low-light advantage and creating a "flat" look.
*   **Ignoring 4:3:** Failing to use the full 4:3 sensor height when shooting anamorphic, resulting in wasted resolution.

**17. Similar Concepts**
ARRI Alexa Mini (Predecessor), ARRI Alexa SXT, Sony Venice (Competitor), RED V-Raptor (Competitor).

**18. Differences from Similar Concepts**
*   **Vs Alexa Mini:** Alexa 35 has 2.5 stops more dynamic range (17 vs 14.5) and a new sensor (ALEV 4 vs ALEV III).
*   **Vs RED V-Raptor:** Alexa 35 has lower resolution (4.6K vs 8K) but generally considered superior highlight rolloff and skin tone rendering.

**19. Compatible Concepts**
*   **Lenses:** ARRI Signature Primes, Cooke S7/i, Zeiss Supreme Primes, Vintage Anamorphics (Panavision C-Series).
*   **Lighting:** Soft, large sources (Book lights, Musco lights) to complement the organic sensor look.
*   **Film Stocks:** Kodak Vision3 emulation (matches the dynamic range curve).

**20. Incompatible Concepts**
*   **Style:** "Broadcast Video" look, hyper-sharp digital aesthetics, high-contrast "crushed" blacks (wastes the sensor's range).
*   **Lenses:** Cheap plastic lenses (the sensor resolves too much detail, exposing lens flaws).

**21. Intensity Levels (Configurations)**
*   **Standard (4.6K):** Maximum quality, 17 stops DR.
*   **4K UHD (Cropped):** Slight crop, standard delivery.
*   **2K ProRes:** Downsampled in-camera for faster workflow (not recommended for VFX).

**22. Photographic Examples**

**23. Cinema Examples**

**24. CGI/VFX Examples**

#### ENTRY 02: ARRI ALEXA MINI LF

**1. Name**
ARRI Alexa Mini LF (Large Format)

**2. Alternative Names**
Mini LF, AMLF, "The Big Sensor".

**3. Category / Subcategory**
Digital Cinema Camera / Large Format (Full Frame) / High-End Production.

**4. Definition**
A compact cinema camera combining the form factor of the Alexa Mini with a Large Format (36x24mm equivalent) version of the ALEV III sensor. It bridges the gap between the classic ARRI look and the immersive depth of Large Format.

**5. Etymology**
*Mini*: Refers to the compact body size.
*LF*: Large Format (Full Frame).

**6. Visual Effect**
Retains the classic "ARRI Look" (colors, skin tones, rolloff) of the original Alexa but with **Large Format characteristics**: shallower depth of field, wider field of view on wider lenses, and a more "immersive" or "3D" stereoscopic feel to the image.

**7. Physical/Technical Mechanism**
Uses the **ALEV III** sensor (same generation as Alexa SXT/Mini) but in a larger **Large Format** size (36.70 x 25.54 mm). It does *not* have the 17 stops of the Alexa 35; it has approx **14.5 stops**. It uses the older **ARRI Color Science** (not REVEAL).

**8. How It Changes the Image**
*   **Depth of Field:** Significantly shallower than Super 35 for the same field of view. Isolates subjects more aggressively.
*   **Perspective:** Allows wider lenses to be used without distortion, creating a more natural human-eye perspective in wide shots.
*   **Resolution:** 4.5K (4448 x 3096). Slightly lower pixel count than Alexa 35 but larger photosites (better light gathering per pixel).

**9. Real-World Origin**
Released in 2019. ARRI's answer to the Sony Venice and RED Monstro 8K VV.

**10. Historical Context**
Created to allow ARRI to compete in the "Large Format" wars (started by the Alexa 65 rental-only camera) without forcing productions to use the massive, heavy Alexa 65 body.

**11. Level of Realism**
**Large Format Cinematic Realism.** High immersion, shallow focus.

**12. Visual Characteristics**
*   **Bokeh:** Creamier, larger out-of-focus highlights compared to Super 35.
*   **Sharpness:** Slightly softer center-to-edge performance than the Alexa 35 due to the older sensor design, but "flattering" for faces.
*   **Colors:** The classic "Alexa" look that defined the 2010s cinema look (*Skyfall*, *Mad Max: Fury Road*).

**13. Advantages**
*   Compact size (fits in tight spaces, cars, drones).
*   Large Format look (immersive).
*   Proven reliability (ALEV III sensor is battle-tested).
*   Matches perfectly with original Alexa Mini for multi-cam shoots.

**14. Disadvantages / Limitations**
*   **Dynamic Range:** 14.5 stops is good, but inferior to the Alexa 35 (17 stops).
*   **Resolution:** 4.5K is lower than RED Monstro (8K).
*   **Older Tech:** Uses the previous generation sensor and color science.

**15. Typical Use Cases**
Feature films requiring Large Format (e.g., *1917*, *The Revenant* style immersion), Steadicam work, car rigs, drone shots.

**16. Common Mistakes**
*   **Using S35 Lenses:** Mounting Super 35 lenses on this camera causes severe vignetting (black corners) unless in "cropped" mode (which defeats the purpose of LF).
*   **Over-sharpening:** The ALEV III is naturally soft; adding digital sharpening creates artifacts.

**17. Similar Concepts**
Sony Venice (1st Gen), RED Monstro 8K VV, ARRI Alexa 65.

**18. Differences from Similar Concepts**
*   **Vs Sony Venice:** Mini LF has better skin tones and build quality, but Venice has Dual Base ISO (better low light) and 6K resolution.
*   **Vs Alexa 65:** Mini LF is smaller, lighter, and available for purchase (not just rental), but has half the resolution.

#### ENTRY 03: ARRI ALEXA 65

**1. Name**
ARRI Alexa 65

**2. Alternative Names**
The Beast, 65mm Digital, IMAX Digital Camera.

**3. Category / Subcategory**
Digital Cinema Camera / 65mm Format / Rental Only / Ultra-High-End.

**4. Definition**
A massive digital cinema camera featuring a **65mm format** sensor (3x the size of a Super 35 sensor). It is composed of three Alexa sensors stitched together vertically. It is available **only via rental** from ARRI Rental.

**5. Etymology**
*65*: Refers to the **65mm film** format it emulates (used for IMAX and large format epics like *Ben-Hur*).

**6. Visual Effect**
**Monumental Scale.** The image has an unparalleled sense of scale and "presence." The depth of field is razor-thin at equivalent focal lengths, creating a stereoscopic 3D effect even in 2D. Resolution is extreme (6.5K), resolving details invisible to the naked eye.

**7. Physical/Technical Mechanism**
Uses a custom **65mm ALEV III** sensor (54.12 x 25.58 mm active area). It is effectively three Alexa sensors arranged vertically.
*   **Resolution:** 6560 x 3100 (6.5K).
*   **Dynamic Range:** 14+ Stops.

**8. How It Changes the Image**
*   **Scale:** Makes landscapes and sets look massive.
*   **Separation:** Subjects are cleanly separated from backgrounds even at medium focal lengths.
*   **Detail:** Captures pore-level detail, requiring extreme care in makeup and set design.

**9. Real-World Origin**
Developed by ARRI Rental in collaboration with IMAX. Used for *Avengers: Infinity War*, *Endgame*, *The Revenant*.

**10. Historical Context**
Created to provide a digital alternative to 65mm film (IMAX) for Hollywood blockbusters that wanted the "IMAX look" without the noise, cost, and bulk of film magazines.

**11. Level of Realism**
**Hyper-Real / Epic Scale.** Beyond human vision; "God's eye view."

**12. Visual Characteristics**
*   **Aspect Ratio:** Native 2.11:1 (often cropped to 2.39:1 or 1.90:1 for IMAX).
*   **Weight:** Massive (approx 10.5 kg body only).
*   **Look:** Clean, clinical, immensely detailed, "stereo" feel.

**13. Advantages**
*   Highest resolution in the ARRI lineup.
*   Largest sensor size (65mm).
*   Matches ARRI color science.

**14. Disadvantages / Limitations**
*   **Availability:** Rental only (cannot buy).
*   **Size/Weight:** Requires heavy-duty heads (OConnor 2575D or similar). Not for handheld.
*   **Data:** Massive data rates.
*   **Cost:** Prohibitive for anything but tentpole blockbusters.

**15. Typical Use Cases**
IMAX blockbusters (*Avengers*, *Transformers*), epic landscapes, VFX-heavy sequences requiring massive resolution.

**16. Common Mistakes**
*   **Makeup:** Standard makeup fails on Alexa 65; requires "HD/UHD" makeup or it looks like mud.
*   **Set Design:** Backgrounds must be detailed; empty walls look fake at this resolution.

**17. Similar Concepts**
IMAX Film Cameras, RED Monstro 8K VV (smaller but high res), Sony Venice (with full frame lenses).

**18. Differences from Similar Concepts**
*   **Vs IMAX Film:** Alexa 65 is quieter, has longer run times, no film grain, but lacks the "organic" chaos of film.
*   **Vs RED Monstro:** Alexa 65 sensor is physically larger (65mm vs VistaVision/Full Frame), offering true medium-format compression.

**19. Compatible Concepts**
*   **Lenses:** ARRI DNA Lenses (rehoused vintage glass to soften the clinical look), Hasselblad XCD, Prime 65 S, Prime 65.
*   **Environments:** Massive sets, landscapes, VFX arenas.

**20. Incompatible Concepts**
*   **Environments:** Small interiors, cars (camera won't fit).
*   **Style:** "Gritty" documentary (too clean, too large).

**21. Intensity Levels (Configurations)**
*   **Full Gate (6.5K):** Maximum resolution.
*   **4K Crop:** For anamorphic or standard delivery.

**22. Photographic Examples**
N/A. *Reference: Large Format Photography (Ansel Adams).*

**23. Cinema Examples**
*   *Avengers: Infinity War / Endgame* (Trent Opaloch) - First major use of Alexa 65 for a full feature.
*   *The Revenant* (Emmanuel Lubezki) - Used for specific wide shots (alongside Alexa M).
*   *Transformers: The Last Knight* (Jonathan Sela).

**24. CGI/VFX Examples**
The standard for "IMAX" VFX shots where the plate needs to be 6K+ to allow for stabilization and reframing.

**25. Prompt Keywords**
`shot on ARRI Alexa 65`, `65mm digital`, `IMAX format`, `medium format cinema`, `extreme resolution`, `monumental scale`, `shallow depth of field`, `ARRI DNA lenses`, `8K upscale`.

**26. Technical Notes**
*   **Mount:** XPL (Hasselblad X-mount / ARRI XPL).
*   **Codex Vault:** Required for offloading data.
*   **Sensor:** 3x ALEV III stitched.

**27. Professional Notes**
"The Alexa 65 is a specialized tool. You don't use it for a dialogue scene in a kitchen. You use it when you want the audience to feel the scale of a mountain or the grandeur of a spaceship. It forces a specific, epic grammar of filmmaking."
